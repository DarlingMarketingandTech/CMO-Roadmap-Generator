# Product and UX brief

Status: proposed direction. Validate with Jacob and target users before substantial redesign.

## Product promise

“A clear next step for your marketing, with a plan you can actually use.” CMO Roadmap Generator can remain the project name; public naming can be tested if owners find CMO jargon unfamiliar. Do not rename it just to signal a new build.

The product is useful when it reduces uncertainty and helps someone do a sensible thing. A long, personalized-looking report is not enough.

## Pilot audience and job

Recommended starting audience: owner-led local/professional businesses with limited marketing capacity. This matches the LLC context and makes strategic review practical. Existing SaaS, ecommerce, healthcare, agency and nonprofit inputs remain baseline features, but do not promise equally deep expertise until the corresponding scenarios/content are reviewed.

Core job: “I have several marketing and systems problems. Help me decide what to fix first with the time and resources I actually have.” Secondary job: “Give me an understandable summary I can discuss with a colleague or strategist.”

## Desired journey

1. Understand the benefit, approximate commitment and how answers are used.
2. Answer a short set of understandable questions with progress, back and clear choices.
3. See an immediate useful recommendation without providing contact information.
4. Review the reason, assumptions and what can wait.
5. Save/share the plan or request one explicit follow-up.
6. Receive an honest acknowledgement and a usable next destination.

## Intake changes to test

- Replace SaaS-centric ARR/age stage definitions for non-SaaS owners.
- Distinguish service category from regulated industry only if it changes the recommendation.
- Test a small weekly-time or readiness question instead of assuming six modules are feasible for a founder.
- Do not ask for exact revenue, detailed customer data or confidential business records unless a proven decision requires them.
- Reconcile contradictory answers by asking a clarifying question or showing an assumption rather than silently selecting one.
- Consider resume/back-to-answer editing after usefulness and privacy behavior are clear.

Every new question must have an explicit decision it changes. Remove questions that only decorate the output.

## Result hierarchy

### First screen

A short situation summary, top priority, one immediate action, expected effort band and a simple definition of done. Explain the recommendation in the owner's words. Keep service sales secondary to actual usefulness.

### Plan detail

For each selected action: why now, prerequisites, who owns it, effort assumptions, success evidence and sequence. Show deferred work and the reason it can wait. Do not label business outcomes as already achieved. Do not claim an exact ROI or statistically derived confidence score from seven categorical answers.

### Optional explanation

Expandable “Why this recommendation?” reveals relevant answers, rule contributions and uncertainty. The default screen should not require understanding flags, schema versions, deterministic internals or API configuration.

### Follow-up

Keep one working “Email my plan” action and a direct approved “Discuss this plan” destination. If a fuller consulting service is offered, explain what is additional; do not imply the free result is incomplete merely to manufacture an upgrade. Remove nonfunctional form choices.

## Accessibility and resilience

Use native radio/checkbox semantics where suitable, grouped labels, clear progress and focus transfer. Support keyboard navigation, readable errors and reduced motion. Keep content readable when animation is unavailable. Provide useful no-JavaScript/error fallbacks where feasible; do not claim them until tested.

Use plain language and strong contrast outdoors/on phones. Test long labels and enlarged text. Respect user choice when copying links fails. Rework print as a readable document with version/date/assumptions; avoid dumping every UI panel into the PDF.

## Brand and website fit

Apply the visual identity of the owner-selected canonical LLC site when integration begins. The current app uses dark/orange styling; this is historical implementation, not a requirement for the next site. Keep domain logic independent of visual tokens and marketing service labels.

## Evidence of value to seek

- Users can explain the first action without help.
- The recommendation fits their constraints and avoids a misleading industry assumption.
- They save/share or act on the plan for a reason they can describe.
- Requests reach an actual durable destination and relevant conversations occur.

Avoid treating email capture, a high completion rate or favorable comments alone as proof that the advice works. A small pilot can reveal useful direction without proving a market or revenue forecast.
